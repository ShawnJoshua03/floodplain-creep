## M1 data access
- Buildings path: public GCS bucket over HTTPS, windowed reads by GDAL `/vsicurl/`. No Earth Engine account needed.
- Buildings are summed from 0.5 m to 4 m pixels while streaming, 512 source rows at a time. Reading the box at full resolution would be about 10 GB per year. Fractional count is summed, so totals are kept. Height and presence use the block maximum.
- `data/raw/buildings/buildings_<year>.tif` is not tied to the box in the file name. If `bbox` in `config/city.yaml` changes, delete these files first.
- Flood path: direct download from the JRC data store, six files for the Nairobi tile (about 77 MB).
- Fetch steps print each raster. Run: `uv run python -m pipeline.steps.fetch_flood` and `uv run python -m pipeline.steps.fetch_buildings [year ...]`. One year takes about 3 minutes.

## M1 finding and city change: Nairobi to Dhaka
- Box 36.7525..36.8875 E, -1.3535..-1.2185 N: for RP10, RP50, RP100 and RP500 the only valid pixels were the same 24 as the permanent water layer, all with depth 1. No modeled river flooding. A wider window had about 66,500 flooded pixels, so the read was right and the layer misses small urban channels.
- 16 cities were scanned for RP100 flooded pixels in a 15 km box. Dhaka centre box: 13,470 of 26,244 pixels.
- Human chose Dhaka. Box first set to 90.3425..90.4775 E, 23.7425..23.8775 N (moved in M2, next section). The Nairobi building rasters were deleted.
- Dhaka sanity check: buildings 390,221 (2016) to 523,511 (2023), +34%. Share of buildings in the RP100 zone 34.8% to 39.4%. The JRC layer marks about 57% of the box as flooded above 0.1 m, so the zone is wide. Read results with the 90 m and river-only limits in mind.
- Boundaries: geoBoundaries ADM3 (thana level), because no open ward polygons were found. Neighbourhood means thana here.

## M2 computation choices
- Box moved south in M2 to 90.3425..90.4775 E, 23.6925..23.8275 N. Root cause: the first box cut off Old Dhaka, so Motijheel, Paltan, Shahbagh, Hazaribagh and Sabujbagh fell under the coverage rule. The new box keeps 33 neighborhoods. Demra, Kadamtali, Keraniganj, Khilkhet, Rupganj, Savar and Turag are dropped (under 50% inside the box). Building rasters were refetched.
- Building count: sum of `building_fractional_count`, kept only where `building_presence` is at least 0.45 (`presence_threshold`). Source: the dataset page says confidence is for relative ranking only and gives no threshold; a published paper used 0.45. We have not read the dataset technical report.
- Threshold sensitivity (first box, 2016 to 2023): 0 gives 390k to 524k (+34%), 0.3 gives +36%, 0.45 gives +39%, 0.6 gives +37%, 0.75 gives +29%. Absolute totals fall from 390k to 167k as the threshold rises. Treat counts as relative, not exact.
- Grid: 100 m cells in EPSG:32646. Buildings are summed from 4 m pixels. Flood depth, permanent water and spurious depth are resampled to the grid with the maximum of the ~90 m source pixels, so thin flooded strips are kept. This leans toward a wider zone.
- Flood zone cell: modeled depth at least `min_depth_m` (0.1, the JRC dataset minimum), not permanent water, not spurious depth.
- Neighborhood = geoBoundaries ADM3 polygon clipped to the box, kept when at least 50% of its area is inside. A cell belongs to the polygon holding its centre.
- Table columns (these replace the PLAN.md names `exposed_buildings` and `total_buildings_in_zone`; use them in the M4 schema): `buildings_in_zone`, `new_buildings_in_zone`, `growth_pct`, `suppressed`, `change_available`.
- K rule: stock below K=20 is suppressed with null counts. New buildings and growth since 2016 are shown only when both the year and the 2016 value reach K. Otherwise a shown change would reveal a hidden 2016 count. This rule is in `pipeline/lib/suppression.py` only, and `check_rows` verifies every row before the CSV is written.

## M2 finding: the 2016 baseline looks unstable
- Citywide buildings in the RP100 zone: 2016 182,749; 2017 205,505; 2018 246,874; 2019 250,035; 2020 261,632; 2021 256,714; 2022 246,922; 2023 263,637.
- Most of the 2016 to 2023 rise (+44%) happens by 2018, then the series is flat or falls. All buildings show the same shape (426k, 470k, 564k, 551k, 579k, 579k, 553k, 581k). Real building growth rarely looks like this. It more likely reflects imagery or model changes, which the dataset page warns about.
- Gendaria shows a decrease (-9) in the zone, another sign of detection noise.
- Effect: "new buildings since 2016" probably overstates real change. Options: keep 2016 and say so on screen, switch the baseline to 2018, or report a smoothed trend. Decide at the gate; M3 validation labels (2016 vs 2023 imagery) will measure how well the change signal matches reality.

## M3 validation design
- Cell change = building count in the last year minus the first year (2023 minus 2016), per 100 m cell, with the same presence mask as the counts.
- Sample: 100 cells drawn at random from the 6,912 flood-zone (RP100) cells inside a kept neighborhood, plus 40 cells drawn at random from the 1,940 cells with an increase of 12 or more buildings. Seed 42. Settings are in `config/validation.yaml`. The 12-building cutoff exists because 62% of zone cells rise by 2 or more, so a low cutoff would flag most cells.
- The labeling sheet (`data/processed/labeling_sheet.csv`) has cell id, place, two links and an empty label. It has no algorithm output and no sample type, and its row order is shuffled.
- Labels: `increase`, `no_clear_change`, `decrease`, `unclear`. Increase is the positive class. `no_clear_change` and `decrease` are negatives. `unclear` cells are dropped and counted.
- Evaluation: the first 60% of labeled cells in sheet order pick the change threshold (best F1, ties go to the lower threshold). Accuracy, precision, recall and Wilson 95% intervals come from the remaining 40% only.
- Limitation: the 40 extra cells make the sample richer in increases than the city. Precision and recall describe the sample. The sampling note says so and travels with the result. Weighting back to the whole city is not done.
- HUMAN: after labeling, write the imagery source and dates in `config/validation.yaml` (`imagery_source`, `imagery_dates`). Evaluation refuses to run while they are blank.

## M3 labeling with an AI reviewer and a human audit
- Why: labeling 140 cells by hand takes hours. The plan allows any dated imagery source, so labeling is split. Hand labeling the whole sheet stays the fallback.
- Imagery: Esri World Imagery Wayback, releases 2017-11-16 and 2024-12-12. The release date is not the capture date. Capture dates are read per cell from the Wayback metadata layers and saved in `data/processed/labeling_captures.csv`. All 140 cells show the same captures: 2017-02-25 (Metro, 0.31 m) and 2023-11-10 (Vivid Advanced, 0.5 m). No 2016 capture exists in the archive for these cells that is closer than 2017-02-25 except 2015-03-22 (0.46 m).
- Effect on the test: the building data compares 2016 with 2023, but the imagery starts in February 2017. Real change during 2016 and early 2017 is invisible to reviewers, so some algorithm increases will look like false positives. This needs weighing when reading precision.
- Reviewer: Claude, from `data/processed/labeling_images/<cell>.png` (before and after, the 100 m square outlined, capture dates in the header). The reviewer did not see the algorithm's per-cell change values. It recorded a confidence per label in `ai_labels.csv`. Rubric: increase means clearly more buildings in the square at the end date, decrease means clearly fewer, no_clear_change means the same buildings or none (vegetation, water and field changes do not count).
- AI labels (140): 50 increase, 87 no_clear_change, 3 decrease. 65 of the no_clear_change labels are low confidence, mostly dense areas where both images are full of roofs and a single added building is hard to see. Expect the reviewer to miss infill there.
- Audit: a random 30 cells (`audit_sheet.csv`, seed + 1) go to a person to label blind. `merge_labels` gives a person's label priority over an audit label, and an audit label priority over an AI label. The result file records the label sources and the AI to human agreement with a Wilson interval.
- If agreement is poor, label the full sheet by hand. A label typed in `labeling_sheet.csv` always wins.

## M3 preliminary result (AI labels only, not audited, do not publish)
- With 140 AI labels: threshold 0.18 buildings, test set 56 cells. Accuracy 0.34 (0.23 to 0.47), precision 0.30 (0.19 to 0.44), recall 0.88 (0.66 to 0.97). Confusion on the test set: 15 true positives, 35 false positives, 2 false negatives, 4 true negatives.
- Reading: the algorithm calls almost every cell an increase, which fits the earlier finding that 62% of zone cells gain 2 or more buildings and that the 2016 baseline looks unstable. The F1-tuned threshold lands near zero for the same reason.
- Caveats: unaudited labels, the 2017 imagery start, and a test set that includes oversampled flagged cells. Treat as a signal to investigate, not as the accuracy number.

## Baseline year changed from 2016 to 2018 (human decision)
- Reason: citywide buildings in the RP100 zone jump from 182,749 (2016) to 246,874 (2018), then stay flat or fall. The first labeling test (AI labels, unaudited) also found the change signal over-calls increases. The 2016 and 2017 layers look unstable, so "since 2016" overstates real change.
- Change: `years: [2018, 2023]` in `config/city.yaml`. Grid, aggregation and suppression follow it. The 2016 and 2017 rasters stay in `data/raw/` and are not used. The table now has 792 rows (33 neighborhoods, 6 years, 4 return periods), 582 shown and 210 suppressed.
- Wording changed to match in CLAUDE.md (product description, time-lapse, limits rule), PLAN.md (M2, M3 human step, M6 labels, demo script), GLOSSARY.md and DEMO.md. The limits text must say that 2016 and 2017 are not used because detections are unstable.
- Result with the new baseline, RP100, 2023: Badda +5,434 (16.2%), Khilgaon +1,689, Sabujbagh +1,006, Hazaribagh +651. Lalbagh (-388), Adabor (-496) and Mohammadpur (-505) show net decreases, which is detection noise or demolished and rebuilt structures. The UI must show a decrease as a number with a sign, not hide it.
- Validation impact: the 140-cell sample and its AI labels used imagery from 2017-02 and a 2016 to 2023 change. They no longer match the algorithm window. Next step: re-render with imagery captured near 2018 (Wayback release 2019-12-12, capture 2018-01-21), relabel, and recompute each cell's change from 2018 to 2023. The preliminary result above is superseded by that run.

## M3 result with the 2018 baseline (AI labels only, not audited)
- Imagery now matches the algorithm window: all 140 cells show 2018-01-21 (Metro, 0.31 m) and 2023-11-10 (Vivid Advanced, 0.5 m). This replaces the 2017-start run above, which is archived as `ai_labels_2017start.csv` and `labeling_images_2017start/`.
- AI labels: 35 increase, 102 no_clear_change, 3 decrease. 78 of the no_clear_change labels are low confidence (dense areas). Against the 2017-start labels, 125 of 140 are unchanged. The 15 that changed were all increases visible in 2017 but already built by 2018, which confirms the baseline effect.
- Sample change values were recomputed for 2018 to 2023 (`rebase_validation_sample`). Only 10 of the 40 "flagged" cells still show +12 or more. The flagged cells were drawn under the old baseline, so they no longer give the positives they were meant to. The sampling note says so.
- Test set (56 cells, threshold tuned on 84 at 1.8 buildings): accuracy 0.55 (0.42 to 0.68), precision 0.30 (0.17 to 0.48), recall 0.69 (0.42 to 0.87). Confusion: 9 true positives, 21 false positives, 4 false negatives, 22 true negatives.
- Descriptive, all 140 cells, no tuning: a cutoff of 0.5 buildings flags 84 cells and finds 31 of 35 increases (precision 0.37). A cutoff of 8 flags 31 and finds 14 (precision 0.45, recall 0.40). A cutoff of 12 flags 20 and finds 8. Precision never passes about 0.45 at any cutoff.
- Reading: building-count change in a single 100 m cell is a weak signal of a visible new building. Roughly half to two thirds of cells the algorithm calls an increase show no new building in the imagery. Cell-level noise partly averages out in neighborhood totals, but this test does not measure that.
- Caveats: labels come from one AI reviewer and are not audited. 78 low-confidence labels sit mostly in dense areas where infill is easy to miss, which would lower measured precision. Precision and recall describe an oversampled sample. Do not publish these numbers. The audit sheet (30 cells) is the next check.
- Consequence for the product: the accuracy card must show these numbers as they are once audited. Copy must say that counts describe modeled building density and are least reliable cell by cell, and that growth is shown by neighborhood only. Rule 5 stands: nothing about accuracy is hardcoded.

## M5 API choices
- Settings: `api/core/settings.py` is the one settings object. It reads `DATABASE_URL`, `config/city.yaml`, `config/banned_terms.txt`, `config/misuse_notice.txt` and the new `config/limits.txt`. It does not import from `pipeline/io`.
- Limits text lives in `config/limits.txt`, one limit per line. It avoids the banned words on purpose. The misuse notice, which is exempt, carries the plain statement about evictions and enforcement. The UI must show both.
- City summary: totals sum only the neighborhoods that are shown. Suppressed neighborhoods are counted (`neighborhoods_suppressed`), never summed, so the total is a floor. The UI must say so.
- Brief: `GET /brief/{id}` returns a stored brief only. A missing brief is a 404. Generation and the templated fallback arrive in M7. A stored brief that holds a banned term is logged and not served.
- Overlay: `render_overlays.py` draws JRC depth in Web Mercator at 100 m, using the same flood-zone rule as the grid (minimum depth, no permanent water, no spurious depth). Images and `overlays.json` go to `data/processed/overlays/`. The API serves only files named in the manifest. The overlay is a hazard layer and holds no building data.
- Validation: `GET /validation` returns the stored payload and `label_sources`, so the UI can say when labels are AI-made and not audited.

## M6b to M6e frontend choices
- Types: `web/src/api/types.ts` renames generated schema types. It holds no hand-copied shapes. There is no runtime schema library in the approved stack, so `client.ts` checks that a response is a JSON object and trusts the generated types for the rest.
- Navigation: `SheetPanel` derives the NavStack from the selection (a neighborhood) plus one local value (the accuracy view), instead of a separate reducer. A reducer would need syncing with the selection on every map click.
- Map: colors come from CSS tokens once, when the map is created. A scheme-change listener is not needed because the app is dark only. Year changes cross-fade through feature state and a paint transition, so the geometry loads once per scenario. The dashed outline for suppressed neighborhoods uses line opacity, because the map library does not allow feature state in filters.
- Color breaks: the five exposure groups come from the last year's shown counts, so one color means the same count in every year.
- Brief: a missing brief is a normal answer (404 in the API), shown as "No written summary yet."
- `CitySummary` now carries `return_periods` and `min_count_k`, so the frontend reads both from config through the API.
- Dev server: Vite proxies the API paths to 127.0.0.1:8000. Hosting is an M8 decision.

## Badda only (human decision, 2026-11-08)
- Reason: across Dhaka, most neighborhood changes sit inside year-to-year detection noise. Badda is the one large case that stands out (smoothed change +9.9% at the 100-year scenario, about 2.1 times the typical swing; 595 flood-zone areas gained 5 or more buildings and 141 lost 5 or more). A visual check of 2018 and 2023 imagery found real land conversion, including a filled pond, but also densification the counts overstate.
- Change: the API serves one neighborhood (`focus_neighborhood` in `config/city.yaml`) through `GET /focus`. The list endpoints are removed, and a test asserts the route list. Other neighborhoods stay in the pipeline and the database only to measure typical noise, and are never named in a response.
- Evidence shown: the smoothed change against the typical yearly swing (`signal_noise_multiple` = 2), and flood-zone areas gaining against losing. The change balance counts 100 m areas as numbers only, with K applied in `suppression.py`.
- Framing risk: one named neighborhood can read as a target list. Mitigation: the misuse notice and limits are one click away, the app says other neighborhoods also gained buildings, and imagery of plots stays in the pitch only, never in the app or the repo.
- Opens on the 10-year scenario, where the signal is strongest (+31.8%, 6.4 times the swing). At 500 years the verdict is within noise (1.8 times). The verdict is shown for every scenario.

## M7 briefs
- Facts: a brief may state only what is in `BriefInput` (`api/schemas/brief_input.py`), built from the computed focus view. The prompt carries a glossary of the fields and rules for plain language, no blame, no links, no field names.
- Checks, in `api/services/brief_writer.py` and `api/safety/`: every number must appear in the input (`number_check.py`), no banned term, 90 words or fewer, one paragraph, a finished sentence, the neighborhood named, no underscore, and the model's `finish_reason` must be STOP. Any failure stores the template instead.
- Fallback and its label: a templated brief from the same facts, stored with source `template`. The UI footnote reads "Written from a fixed template and the numbers above." A Gemini brief reads "Written from the numbers above. Numbers checked."
- Official pointers (at most two, from `config/resources.yaml`) are added by code after the checks, never written by the model. The file is empty until the human fills it.
- Measured on 2026-11-08 with `gemini-3.5-flash`: 3 of 4 briefs pass per run. The rest fall back, usually for length or an added number. That is the checks working.
- Windows gotcha: a stale user-level `GEMINI_API_KEY` environment variable overrides `.env`, because `uv run --env-file` does not override variables that already exist.
